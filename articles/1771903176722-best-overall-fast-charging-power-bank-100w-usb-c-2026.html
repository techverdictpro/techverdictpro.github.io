Comprehensive Buying Guide

For laptop users, calculate your device's battery capacity and multiply by your desired number of full charges, then add 30% for conversion losses and smartphone charging. Frequently Asked Questions

Can 100W power banks damage my laptop or smartphone?
Modern devices include sophisticated charging controllers that negotiate appropriate power levels. A 100W power bank will only deliver the wattage your device requests, making it safe for smartphones that typically draw 20-30W maximum.

How long do 100W power banks take to recharge?
With a 100W charger, most 25,000-30,000mAh power banks require 90-120 minutes for complete recharging. Lower-wattage chargers proportionally increase charging time.

Can I bring these power banks on airplanes?
All power banks under 100Wh (approximately 27,000mAh) are permitted in carry-on luggage. Larger capacities require airline approval and must remain in carry-on bags, never checked luggage.

Why don't these power banks fully charge my laptop's advertised capacity?
Energy conversion losses, heat generation, and power bank efficiency typically result in 70-80% of rated capacity reaching your device. This is normal and expected across all brands.

How often should I fully drain my power bank?
Modern lithium polymer batteries prefer partial charge cycles. Fully draining the battery monthly helps calibrate the capacity meter but isn't necessary for battery health.

What's the difference between 100W USB-C and USB-C PD?
USB-C Power Delivery (PD) is the protocol that enables high-wattage charging. All 100W USB-C ports use PD, but not all USB-C PD ports support 100W – many are limited to 18W, 30W, or 65W.
