Buying Guide: Choosing the Right Capture System

Selecting the optimal multi-input capture system depends on several critical factors that vary significantly between broadcast environments. Understanding these key considerations will help you choose the solution that best matches your specific workflow requirements.

Input Requirements: Count your maximum simultaneous inputs, including cameras, computer screens, and external devices. Consider both current needs and near-term expansion plans. The ATEM Mini Pro ISO handles four inputs natively, while PCIe-based solutions can often accommodate more inputs through multiple cards.

Resolution and Frame Rate: Determine whether 1080p60 meets your quality requirements or if 4K capture is necessary. Higher resolutions significantly impact storage requirements and processing demands. Consider that many streaming platforms still optimize for 1080p delivery, making 4K capture primarily valuable for post-production flexibility.

Standalone vs. PC-Based: Standalone switchers like the ATEM Mini Pro ISO offer simplicity and reliability but limit customization options. PC-based capture cards provide more flexibility and processing power but introduce additional complexity and potential failure points. Consider your technical expertise and preference for hands-on management.

Audio Handling: Evaluate your audio workflow complexity. Simple embedded HDMI audio may suffice for basic productions, while complex shows might require dedicated audio interfaces and mixing capabilities. Some capture systems include professional audio processing features that eliminate the need for separate audio equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can these capture systems handle live streaming and recording simultaneously?

Most modern capture systems support simultaneous streaming and recording, but implementation varies. The ATEM Mini Pro ISO can stream via Ethernet while recording to USB storage. PC-based solutions typically offer more flexibility for multiple simultaneous outputs but require adequate system resources.

What computer specifications are required for multi-input capture?

Requirements vary by solution complexity. Standalone switchers need minimal computer resources, while PCIe capture cards typically require modern CPUs (Intel i5/AMD Ryzen 5 or better), 16GB+ RAM, and dedicated graphics cards for real-time effects. SSD storage is essential for multi-stream recording.

Do I need special cables or adapters for professional cameras?

Most prosumer cameras output HDMI, which these systems handle natively. Professional cameras with SDI outputs require HDMI converters. Some cameras may need specific settings enabled for clean HDMI output without overlays or menus.

How much storage space does multi-input recording require?

Storage requirements scale with input count and quality settings. Four 1080p60 streams typically consume 20-30GB per hour when using efficient codecs. Plan for 2-3x your estimated needs to accommodate varying content complexity and quality fluctuations.

Can these systems integrate with existing broadcast equipment?

Integration depends on your current setup. HDMI-based systems require converters for SDI workflows, while some solutions offer more professional connectivity options. Consider existing audio infrastructure and monitoring requirements when planning integration.

What's the learning curve for switching from single-camera setups?

The transition complexity varies by chosen solution. Standalone switchers typically require 1-2 hours to master basic operations, while software-based systems may need several days of practice to achieve smooth operation. Factor in time for testing and backup procedure development.

Are there ongoing costs beyond the initial purchase?

Most capture systems have minimal ongoing costs. Consider storage media replacement, potential software subscription fees, and occasional firmware updates. Cloud streaming services may incur monthly fees depending on your distribution strategy.
