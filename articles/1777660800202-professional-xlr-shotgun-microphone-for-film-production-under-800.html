Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need phantom power for XLR shotgun microphones?

Yes, all condenser shotgun microphones require phantom power, typically 48V supplied by your audio recorder or camera. This powers the microphone's internal electronics and is standard on professional equipment.

How important is wind protection for outdoor filming?

Extremely important. Even light breezes can create unusable audio without proper wind protection. Always use the included windscreen outdoors, and consider investing in a professional windshield system for serious outdoor work.

Can I use these microphones directly with DSLR cameras?

Most modern DSLR cameras with XLR inputs will work fine. However, consumer cameras may require XLR-to-3.5mm adapters or external preamps to provide proper phantom power and gain levels.

What's the difference between shotgun and other microphone types?

Shotgun microphones use interference tube design to create highly directional pickup patterns. This allows you to capture audio from specific sources while rejecting sound from the sides and rear, essential for film work.

How far away can subjects be from the microphone?

For best results, keep subjects within 2-3 feet of the microphone. While shotgun mics are directional, they're not telephoto lenses for audio. Closer placement always yields better signal-to-noise ratios.

Should I buy additional accessories?

A quality boom pole and shock mount are essential for professional results. Wind protection, extension cables, and backup batteries for your recorder round out a complete audio kit.

How do I maintain these microphones properly?

Store in protective cases when not in use, avoid extreme temperatures and humidity, and clean the grille gently with a soft brush. Never use compressed air directly on the diaphragm, as this can cause permanent damage.
