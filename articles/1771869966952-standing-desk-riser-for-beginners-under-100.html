## Buying Guide: Choosing Your First Standing Desk Riser ### Key Specifications to Consider **Height Range**: Ensure the riser's adjustment range accommodates your seated and standing positions. Most users require 12-18 inches of travel, but individual proportions vary. Measure from your current desk surface to your ideal monitor and keyboard heights while standing. **Weight Capacity**: Calculate your equipment's combined weight, including monitors, laptops, keyboards, and accessories. Budget risers typically support 25-40 pounds—sufficient for most setups but potentially limiting for dual-monitor configurations or heavy all-in-one computers. **Surface Dimensions**: Consider your current equipment layout and future expansion plans. Dual-platform designs offer dedicated keyboard space but reduce monitor real estate. Single surfaces provide flexibility but require careful equipment arrangement. **Adjustment Mechanism**: Pneumatic systems balance smooth operation with affordability. Electric options add convenience but increase complexity and cost. Manual mechanisms work reliably but require more physical effort during transitions. ### Workspace Compatibility Assessment Measure your existing desk depth before purchasing. Most risers require 24-30 inches of depth when fully extended, potentially overhanging smaller desks. Consider clearance above your workspace—ceiling fans, shelving, or overhead storage may limit maximum height usage. Cable management becomes critical with height-adjustable equipment. Look for integrated cable routing or plan for aftermarket solutions. Power strips mounted under your desk simplify cord organization during height transitions. ### Budget Optimization Strategies Entry-level risers under $60 often compromise on build quality or features that impact long-term satisfaction. The $80-100 range typically delivers better materials, smoother mechanisms, and improved durability. Consider used or refurbished options from reputable manufacturers. Office furniture liquidations often include barely-used standing equipment at significant discounts. Factor in accessory costs—monitor arms, keyboard trays, or cable management solutions may be necessary depending on your specific setup and the riser's included features.

## Frequently Asked Questions **How much height adjustment do I actually need?** Most users require 10-16 inches of total travel. Measure from your seated keyboard position to your standing position (elbows at 90 degrees). Add 2-3 inches for flexibility and different shoe heights. **Will a riser work with my existing monitor arm?** Monitor arms add weight and leverage forces that may exceed the riser's capacity. Check weight limits and consider the arm's clamp compatibility with the riser's surface thickness. **How stable are budget risers during typing?** Quality risers under $100 exhibit minimal wobble during normal typing and mouse use. Excessive movement usually indicates improper assembly, overloading, or a defective unit. **Can I use a riser with a corner desk?** Corner desk compatibility depends on the available straight-edge space. Most risers require 28-32 inches of linear desk edge. Measure carefully and consider angled placement if necessary. **Do I need a keyboard tray or single surface?** Keyboard trays maintain optimal ergonomic positioning between keyboard and monitor heights. Single surfaces offer more flexibility but may require external keyboard trays or monitor arms for proper ergonomics. **How long do budget risers typically last?** Quality models from established manufacturers often provide 2-3 years of daily use before showing significant wear. Pneumatic mechanisms and surface finishes typically fail before structural components. **What's the return policy if the riser doesn't work for me?** Most major retailers offer 30-day return windows.
