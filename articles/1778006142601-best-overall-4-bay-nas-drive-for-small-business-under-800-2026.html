How to Choose the Right NAS Drive

Capacity planning should drive your decision. Calculate your current data storage needs, then multiply by three to account for growth and redundancy. Most small businesses find 8-16TB of usable storage sufficient for 3-5 years.

Consider your technical expertise honestly. Synology offers the smoothest experience for non-technical users, while QNAP provides more power-user features. TerraMaster hits the middle ground with decent software at budget pricing.

Network infrastructure matters more than many realize. All our picks work fine on standard Gigabit networks, but businesses planning network upgrades should consider models with 2.5GbE or 10GbE ports.

Budget beyond the NAS unit itself. You'll need hard drives, which can easily cost $400-600 for a 4-bay setup. Enterprise or NAS-specific drives like WD Red or Seagate IronWolf offer better reliability than desktop drives.

Backup strategy integration should influence your choice. Look for units with cloud sync capabilities if you use services like Google Workspace or Microsoft 365. Local backup features matter for businesses with existing backup workflows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between RAID levels for business use?

RAID 5 offers the best balance of protection and capacity, giving you three drives' worth of storage with protection against any single drive failure. RAID 6 protects against two simultaneous failures but reduces usable capacity.

Do I need to buy special hard drives for my NAS?

Yes, NAS-optimized drives like WD Red or Seagate IronWolf handle 24/7 operation better than desktop drives. They include vibration compensation and longer warranties designed for multi-drive environments.

Can I access my files remotely while traveling?

All our recommended models support secure remote access through mobile apps and web browsers. Synology's QuickConnect is particularly user-friendly, while QNAP offers more advanced VPN options.

How much power do these units consume?

Expect 30-50 watts during normal operation, similar to a bright LED light bulb. Power consumption varies based on the number of installed drives and system activity levels.

Should I get a UPS for my NAS?

Absolutely. An uninterruptible power supply protects against data corruption during power outages and gives the system time to shut down safely. Budget $100-150 for an appropriate UPS unit.

How often should I replace the hard drives?

NAS drives typically last 3-5 years in business environments. Monitor drive health through the NAS software and replace drives showing warning signs before they fail completely.

Can I upgrade the RAM in these systems?

Most models allow RAM upgrades, though Synology sometimes voids warranties for non-approved modules. Check manufacturer specifications before purchasing additional memory.
