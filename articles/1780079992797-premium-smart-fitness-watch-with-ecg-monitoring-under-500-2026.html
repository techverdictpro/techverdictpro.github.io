Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate are consumer ECG readings compared to medical devices?

Modern fitness watches achieve 95-99% accuracy compared to clinical ECG machines for basic rhythm detection. However, they cannot replace professional medical evaluation and should complement, not substitute, regular healthcare checkups.

Can these watches detect heart attacks?

No, consumer ECG watches detect rhythm irregularities like atrial fibrillation but cannot diagnose heart attacks. They monitor electrical activity patterns, not blood flow blockages that cause heart attacks.

How often should I take ECG readings?

Most manufacturers recommend 1-2 readings daily for baseline monitoring, with additional readings if you feel symptoms. Excessive testing doesn't provide additional benefits and may increase anxiety about normal variations.

Do ECG watches work with pacemakers?

ECG functionality may not work properly with pacemakers due to artificial electrical signals. Consult your cardiologist before using these devices if you have any implanted cardiac devices.

What's the difference between heart rate monitoring and ECG?

Heart rate monitoring measures beats per minute using optical sensors, while ECG records the heart's electrical activity patterns. ECG provides more detailed information about rhythm irregularities that simple heart rate monitoring might miss.

Can I share ECG data with my doctor?

Yes, all tested watches allow exporting ECG readings as PDFs or sharing directly through health apps. Many cardiologists now accept consumer ECG data as supplementary information between appointments.

How long do ECG readings take?

Most watches require 30-60 seconds of finger contact with sensors for accurate readings. Some advanced models can perform background ECG monitoring during workouts for continuous rhythm assessment.
