Best Budget 75% Mechanical Keyboards Under $80 in 2026

Introduction

The 75% mechanical keyboard format has become the sweet spot for enthusiasts who want more functionality than a 60% board without the bulk of a full-size keyboard. These compact powerhouses retain the F-row keys while maintaining a sleek, desk-friendly footprint that's perfect for both gaming and productivity work.

The budget segment under $80 has exploded with quality options in 2026, making it easier than ever to get premium features like hot-swappable switches, gasket mounting, and south-facing PCBs without breaking the bank. Gone are the days when budget meant compromise on build quality or typing experience.

We've spent months testing the latest budget 75% keyboards, putting each through rigorous typing tests, gaming sessions, and build quality assessments. Our testing focused on switch feel, stabilizer quality, keycap durability, and overall value proposition.

This review covers our top pick plus three strong alternatives, giving you options whether you prioritize wireless connectivity, specific switch types, or premium build materials within the budget constraint.

Our Top Pick: Keychron V1

The Keychron V1 stands out as our top budget pick thanks to its exceptional build quality that rivals keyboards costing twice as much. The gasket mount design provides a premium typing feel with just the right amount of flex, while the CNC-machined aluminum frame feels rock-solid on your desk.

What sets the V1 apart is its attention to detail in areas where budget keyboards typically cut corners. The plate-mounted stabilizers come pre-lubed and tuned, eliminating the rattly spacebar that plagues cheaper alternatives. The south-facing PCB ensures compatibility with Cherry profile keycaps and provides better RGB underglow.

The hot-swappable 3-pin and 5-pin switch sockets use Kailh's reliable design, supporting virtually any MX-style switch on the market. We tested it with everything from linear Gaterons to tactile Zealios, and switch installation was consistently smooth and secure. The PCB includes thoughtful touches like per-key RGB and VIA compatibility for easy remapping.

Typing on the V1 feels premium thanks to the combination of the gasket mount, quality stabilizers, and included Keychron K Pro switches. The acoustics are well-dampened without feeling muted, producing a satisfying thock that's office-appropriate. Build quality is excellent, with no flex in the aluminum case and perfectly aligned switches.

At $75, the V1 hits the sweet spot for enthusiasts who want to upgrade components over time while getting excellent performance out of the box.

Alternative Options Worth Considering

Epomaker TH80 Pro

The TH80 Pro earns its spot as our wireless pick, offering tri-mode connectivity (USB-C, 2.4GHz, Bluetooth 5.0) in a premium aluminum package for just $69. The 4000mAh battery provides impressive longevity, easily lasting two weeks of heavy use with RGB enabled. Wireless performance is excellent, with no noticeable latency in our gaming tests.

The gasket mount implementation feels slightly firmer than the Keychron V1 but still provides good typing dynamics. Epomaker includes foam dampening in both the case and plate, resulting in a deeper, more muted sound signature that some users will prefer for office environments.

The main trade-offs are slightly lower keycap quality (thin ABS versus Keychron's thicker PBT) and stabilizers that benefit from additional lubing. However, the wireless convenience and competitive pricing make it an excellent choice for users who prioritize portability and desk aesthetics.

How to Choose the Right 75% Mechanical Keyboard

Your switch preference should guide your decision between these options. The Keychron V1 and Epomaker TH80 Pro come with quality stock switches that most users will enjoy long-term. The GMMK Pro 75 requires separate switch purchases but offers unlimited customization potential.

Consider your workspace requirements carefully. If you need wireless connectivity for a clean desk setup or travel use, both the TH80 Pro and RK84 deliver reliable wireless performance. Wired-only users can focus purely on build quality and typing feel with the V1 or GMMK Pro.

Budget flexibility matters more than you might think. Starting with the RK84 leaves room for switch and keycap upgrades while staying under $80 total. The other options use most of your budget upfront but deliver better out-of-box experiences.

Build quality expectations should align with your price point. The aluminum-cased options (V1, TH80 Pro, GMMK Pro) feel significantly more premium than the plastic RK84, but all offer satisfying typing experiences when properly set up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes 75% keyboards special compared to other layouts?

The 75% layout keeps the essential F-row keys while eliminating wasted space between key clusters, resulting in a compact board that maintains full functionality for gaming and productivity work.

Do these budget keyboards support custom keycaps?

Yes, all our recommendations use standard Cherry MX-style switches and keycap compatibility. The Keychron V1 and GMMK Pro have south-facing switches for optimal Cherry profile compatibility.

How difficult is it to modify these keyboards?

Most require only basic tools for switch swapping and stabilizer maintenance. The hot-swap sockets make switch changes tool-free, while stabilizer work needs a keycap puller and small screwdriver.

Which keyboard offers the best wireless experience?

The Epomaker TH80 Pro provides the most reliable wireless performance with excellent battery life and tri-mode connectivity options including both Bluetooth and 2.4GHz wireless.

Are the included switches worth keeping or should I upgrade immediately?

The Keychron K Pro and Epomaker switches included with our top picks are genuinely good and worth using long-term. The RK switches are adequate but benefit from eventual upgrades.

How do these compare to more expensive enthusiast keyboards?

Build quality and typing feel are surprisingly close to $150+ keyboards, with the main differences being premium materials, more advanced mounting systems, and included accessories.

What tools do I need for basic maintenance and modding?

A keycap puller, switch puller (if hot-swappable), small Phillips screwdriver, and dielectric grease for stabilizers cover most maintenance needs.
