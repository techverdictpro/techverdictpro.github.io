How to Choose the Right USB-C Hub

Selecting the right Thunderbolt 4 hub depends on your specific workflow and connectivity needs. Start by inventorying your current peripherals and considering future expansion plans—buying too few ports leads to daisy-chaining additional hubs.

Display requirements significantly impact your choice. If you're driving a single 8K monitor, any of our recommendations will work. Multiple high-resolution displays or high refresh rate gaming setups require more careful consideration of the hub's display controller capabilities.

Power delivery matters more than many users realize. Ensure your chosen hub can provide sufficient wattage for your laptop while powering connected peripherals. Most modern laptops need 65-100W, leaving limited headroom for power-hungry devices.

Budget-conscious buyers should prioritize the Plugable TBT4-UDZ for essential features at $199. Power users benefit from the comprehensive connectivity of the CalDigit TS4 at $289. The Anker PowerExpand Elite strikes the best balance for most users at $279.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do these hubs work with non-Thunderbolt devices?

Yes, all Thunderbolt 4 hubs maintain backward compatibility with USB-C devices. However, you'll be limited to USB speeds and power delivery rather than full Thunderbolt performance.

Can I daisy-chain multiple Thunderbolt 4 hubs?

Technically yes, but it's rarely practical. The Thunderbolt 4 specification allows up to six devices in a chain, but bandwidth and power limitations make this scenario challenging for high-performance applications.

How much power do these hubs consume?

Most Thunderbolt 4 hubs consume 15-25W for their own operation, reducing available power delivery to your laptop. Factor this into your power budget calculations.

Are these hubs compatible with gaming laptops?

Absolutely, though gaming laptops often have higher power requirements that may limit available wattage for the hub's operation. Check your laptop's power needs before purchasing.

Can I use multiple 8K displays simultaneously?

This depends on your source device's capabilities rather than the hub itself. Most current laptops support either one 8K display or two 4K displays through a Thunderbolt 4 hub.

Do I need special cables for 8K support?

Yes, 8K@60Hz requires certified Thunderbolt 4 or USB4 cables. Standard USB-C cables won't support the necessary bandwidth for full 8K performance.

How hot do these hubs get during operation?

Modern Thunderbolt 4 hubs manage heat well, typically staying warm but not uncomfortably hot during normal operation. Sustained 8K video processing may increase temperatures noticeably.
