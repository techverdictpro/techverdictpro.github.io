How to Choose the Right NAS System

Capacity planning should be your starting point. Calculate current storage needs, then multiply by three to account for growth, redundancy, and backup copies. An 8-bay system with 4TB drives provides roughly 24TB of usable space in RAID 6 configuration.

Performance requirements vary dramatically by use case. File sharing for office documents needs less bandwidth than video editing workflows. Consider both sequential transfer speeds and concurrent user limits. Look for systems with multiple Gigabit Ethernet ports at minimum.

Software ecosystem often determines long-term satisfaction. Synology and QNAP offer extensive app libraries, while Buffalo focuses on core NAS functionality. Consider whether you need features like surveillance station, backup software, or cloud synchronization built-in.

Budget should include drives, extended warranties, and potential network upgrades. Factor in the cost of enterprise-grade hard drives, which often cost 50% more than consumer versions but offer better reliability and longer warranties for 24/7 operation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between business and consumer NAS systems?

Enterprise NAS systems feature ECC memory, hot-swappable drives, redundant power supplies, and longer warranties. They're designed for 24/7 operation with better cooling and more robust components than consumer models.

How many drives should I populate initially?

Start with at least four drives to enable RAID 6 protection. This allows two drive failures without data loss while providing reasonable performance. You can add drives later as storage needs grow.

Do I need 10 Gigabit Ethernet?

For most small businesses, Gigabit Ethernet provides adequate performance. Upgrade to 10GbE if you regularly transfer large files, have many concurrent users, or run bandwidth-intensive applications like video surveillance.

What RAID level should I use?

RAID 6 offers the best balance of protection and usable space for business use. It survives two drive failures and provides good performance. RAID 10 offers better performance but uses 50% of raw capacity for redundancy.

How often should I replace drives?

Monitor drive health through the NAS management software. Replace drives showing warning signs immediately. For preventive replacement, consider refreshing drives every 4-5 years depending on usage intensity and warranty coverage.

Can I use consumer hard drives?

While consumer drives work, enterprise or NAS-specific drives are recommended. They're designed for continuous operation, include vibration resistance, and typically offer longer warranties and better error recovery for RAID environments.

What backup strategy should I implement?

Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of data, on two different media types, with one copy offsite. Use the NAS for primary storage and local backup, plus cloud backup or offsite replication for disaster recovery.
