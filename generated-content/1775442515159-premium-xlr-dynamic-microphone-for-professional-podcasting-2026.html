How to Choose the Right Microphones

Your recording environment plays the biggest role in microphone selection. If you're recording in an untreated room with background noise, prioritize dynamic microphones with tight pickup patterns like our top picks.

Consider your audio interface's preamp quality and gain capabilities. The SM7B and RE20 require substantial clean gain, while the AT2040USB and PodMic are more forgiving with budget interfaces.

Budget recommendations break down clearly: Under $250, choose the Rode PodMic. Between $250-350, the Audio-Technica AT2040USB offers the best versatility. Above $350, both the SM7B and RE20 deliver professional broadcast quality.

Your voice characteristics matter too. Deeper voices often sound great on the PodMic or RE20, while brighter voices may prefer the AT2040USB. The SM7B works well with virtually any voice type but requires proper gain staging.

Think about future needs. If you plan to expand into video, streaming, or professional production, investing in a SM7B or RE20 makes sense. For dedicated podcasting, any of these options will serve you well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an audio interface with these microphones?

Yes, XLR microphones require an audio interface or mixer to connect to your computer. The Audio-Technica AT2040USB is the exception, offering both XLR and direct USB connectivity.

How much gain do these microphones need?

Dynamic microphones typically require 40-60dB of clean gain. The SM7B needs the most (around 59dB), while the AT2040USB requires the least (around 45dB).

Should I buy a cloudlifter or gain booster?

A cloudlifter helps if your interface lacks sufficient clean gain, particularly with the SM7B. Most modern interfaces provide enough gain for the AT2040USB and PodMic without additional amplification.

What accessories do I need?

A boom arm, shock mount (if not built-in), and pop filter are essential. Some microphones like the SM7B include built-in pop filtering and shock mounting.

How important is the frequency response?

For podcasting, look for microphones with presence boosts around 5-7kHz for voice clarity and controlled low-end to reduce room noise and proximity effect.

Can I use these microphones for music recording?

While designed for voice, these microphones work well for vocals, guitar amps, and drums. The RE20 and SM7B are particularly popular in music production.

How do I reduce background noise?

Choose microphones with tight cardioid patterns, position them close to your mouth (4-6 inches), and consider acoustic treatment for your recording space.
