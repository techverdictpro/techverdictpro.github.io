How to Choose the Right NAS drives

Capacity requirements should drive your initial decision. Calculate current storage needs, then multiply by three to account for growth and redundancy. Most video studios find 144TB to 192TB of raw capacity provides comfortable headroom for active projects.

Performance needs vary dramatically by workflow type. 1080p projects can work with entry-level units, while 4K demands mid-range performance. 8K workflows absolutely require high-end processors and maximum RAM configurations. Don't skimp on specs if you plan to upgrade video quality.

Budget considerations extend beyond the initial purchase price. Factor in drive costs, potential RAM upgrades, and ongoing power consumption. A slightly more expensive unit with better performance often provides superior long-term value through increased productivity.

Network infrastructure plays a crucial role in real-world performance. Gigabit Ethernet becomes a bottleneck quickly with high-resolution video. Budget for 10GbE networking upgrades if you're serious about maximizing performance potential.

Redundancy requirements depend on your backup strategy and risk tolerance. RAID 6 provides excellent protection against drive failures, while RAID 10 offers maximum performance with good redundancy. Plan your RAID configuration before making hardware decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many drives should I populate initially?

Start with at least 8 drives to enable proper RAID configurations. This provides good performance while leaving room for capacity expansion as projects grow. Avoid populating just 4-6 bays initially, as performance suffers significantly.

Can these units handle 8K workflows?

Higher-end models like the QNAP TS-1232PX-4U handle 8K adequately with proper configuration. Budget units struggle with high-bitrate 8K content. Consider proxy workflows if you're using entry-level hardware for 8K projects.

What's the difference between 1U and 2U chassis?

1U units offer maximum rack density but may have limited cooling and expansion options. 2U units provide better airflow and often include more features. Choose based on your rack space constraints and cooling requirements.

Do I need redundant power supplies?

Redundant power is essential for studios where downtime costs money. The relatively small upcharge for dual power supplies pays for itself by preventing costly project delays. Consider it mandatory for professional environments.

How important is RAM capacity?

RAM directly impacts concurrent user performance and caching effectiveness. Start with at least 8GB for basic video work. Studios handling multiple simultaneous editors should budget for 16GB or more to prevent performance bottlenecks.

Should I buy drives separately or as a bundle?

Purchasing drives separately often saves money and provides more flexibility in capacity choices. However, pre-configured bundles offer convenience and guaranteed compatibility. Calculate costs both ways before deciding.

What network connection do I really need?

Gigabit Ethernet works for basic 1080p workflows but becomes limiting quickly. 10GbE provides the headroom professional video demands. Budget for network infrastructure upgrades alongside your NAS investment for best results.
